Why Millet Cookies Are Healthier Than Maida Biscuits

Published on March 14, 2026 • 6 min read • Category: Millet Nutrition

Cookies are one of the most popular tea-time snacks around the world. However, most biscuits available in the market are made using maida (refined wheat flour). While these biscuits may taste good, refined flour loses a large portion of its natural nutrients during processing and mainly provides simple carbohydrates.

Millet cookies offer a different approach to snacking. Instead of refined flour, they are made from traditional whole grains such as ragi (finger millet), jowar (sorghum), and bajra (pearl millet). These grains have been part of traditional diets in many regions for centuries and are valued for their fiber, mineral content, and balanced nutritional profile.

Because of this, many people today are choosing millet-based cookies instead of maida biscuits. These cookies provide a more wholesome alternative for those looking for healthy cookies made without refined flour while still enjoying a familiar snack.

1. Understanding Millets: Traditional Grains with Modern Relevance

Millets are a group of small-seeded cereal grains that have been cultivated for thousands of years across Asia and Africa. Some of the most commonly consumed millets include ragi (finger millet), jowar (sorghum), and bajra (pearl millet). Other varieties such as foxtail millet, little millet, and kodo millet are also part of traditional diets.

These grains are valued because they are typically eaten as whole grains. Unlike refined flour, which removes the bran and germ during processing, whole grains retain more of their natural fiber and micronutrients.

2. Millets Provide More Natural Nutrients

One of the main differences between millet cookies and maida biscuits lies in their nutritional composition. Millets are whole grains, meaning they retain their bran, germ, and endosperm. This allows them to naturally contain higher amounts of fiber, vitamins, and minerals compared to refined flour.

Different millets contribute different nutrients. For example:

  • Ragi (Finger Millet) is widely known for its naturally high calcium content.
  • Jowar (Sorghum) provides antioxidants and plant compounds that support overall nutrition.
  • Bajra (Pearl Millet) contains iron, magnesium, and protein.
  • Other millets such as foxtail millet, little millet, and kodo millet also contribute fiber and micronutrients.

When these grains are used in baking, millet cookies can offer a broader nutritional profile than biscuits made from refined wheat flour alone.

3. Higher Dietary Fiber for Better Satiety

Millets are naturally rich in dietary fiber, which plays an important role in digestion and overall dietary balance. Fiber slows down digestion slightly, which can help you feel full for longer after eating.

Refined flour, on the other hand, contains very little fiber because the bran layer of the grain is removed during processing. As a result, snacks made primarily from maida may not provide the same feeling of satiety.

Millet cookies made with whole grains therefore tend to be a more balanced snack option, especially when paired with tea or coffee during the day.

4. Slower Energy Release Compared to Refined Flour

Another difference between whole grains and refined flour is how they release energy during digestion. Millets generally have a moderate glycemic index, which means they release carbohydrates more gradually into the bloodstream.

Refined flour products are typically digested more quickly, which can sometimes lead to faster spikes and drops in energy levels. Foods made from whole grains such as millets tend to provide a more steady release of energy.

This is one reason why millets are often included in balanced diets that emphasize whole grains over refined ingredients.

5. Traditional Grains Returning to Modern Diets

Millets have been cultivated and consumed in India and many other parts of the world for thousands of years. Before the widespread use of refined flour, many traditional meals included grains such as ragi, jowar, and bajra in everyday cooking.

In recent years, interest in millets has grown again as people look for less processed, more traditional ingredients. Millets are valued for their adaptability, sustainability, and nutritional qualities.

Millet cookies represent a simple way of bringing these traditional grains into modern snacking habits while still enjoying familiar foods like biscuits and cookies.

Millet Cookies vs Maida Biscuits: Which Is Healthier?

Both millet cookies and maida biscuits can be enjoyed as occasional snacks, but their ingredients make an important difference. Cookies made with whole grains such as ragi, jowar, and bajra retain more of the grain's natural nutrients compared to refined flour products.

For people who want to reduce refined ingredients in their diet, choosing snacks made with whole grains can be a small but meaningful step toward more balanced eating.

  • Jowar contains antioxidants and plant compounds.
  • Bajra is rich in iron and magnesium.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are millet cookies healthier than maida biscuits?

Millet cookies made from whole grains typically contain more fiber and micronutrients than biscuits made from refined flour. This makes them a more wholesome snack option when enjoyed in moderation.

Do millet cookies contain gluten?

Most millets such as ragi, jowar, and bajra are naturally gluten-free. However, the final gluten content of cookies depends on the ingredients used in the recipe and whether wheat flour is included.

Why are millets considered nutritious?

Millets are whole grains that naturally contain fiber, plant compounds, and important minerals such as calcium, iron, and magnesium. Because they undergo less processing compared to refined flour, they retain more of their original nutritional value.

What are the benefits of millet cookies?

Millet cookies made with grains like ragi, jowar, and bajra provide fiber, minerals, and whole-grain nutrition compared to cookies made with refined flour.

If you prefer snacks made from whole grains instead of refined flour, millet cookies can be a thoughtful alternative. Grains such as ragi, jowar, and bajra naturally contain fiber and minerals that refined wheat flour often loses during processing. For those looking to move away from traditional maida-based biscuits while still enjoying a satisfying tea-time cookie, millet cookies offer a simple shift toward more wholesome ingredients.
